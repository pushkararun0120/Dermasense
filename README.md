# Dermasense — A pH-Responsive Hydrogel Wound-Dressing Concept

Dermasense is an academic product-design and research project exploring a hydrogel wound dressing with a pH-responsive colour indicator. The idea is to make changes in wound-fluid pH visible without using a battery or electronic sensor.

> **Project status:** Concept and research stage. The current project material includes design mock-ups and research notes, but no validated working prototype, clinical data, or regulatory approval. Dermasense is not intended for medical use or for diagnosing an infection.

## 1. Project Overview

Monitoring a wound at home after surgery can be difficult for patients and caregivers. A dressing covers the wound, and changes in the wound environment may not be obvious without checking it. Follow-up visits can also be inconvenient for people who live far from a healthcare facility.

Dermasense explores whether a visible colour response to changes in wound-fluid pH could provide an additional cue during wound monitoring. The project focuses on the product concept, the research behind pH-responsive materials, possible hydrogel and indicator choices, and the testing that would be needed before the idea could be considered for real-world use.

The aim is to investigate a simple, electronics-free visual monitoring aid. Reducing hospital visits, preventing infection, lowering costs, or improving outcomes are **not established benefits** of the current concept and would need evidence before any such claims could be made.

## 2. Problem Being Explored

The project began with the following problem questions:

- How could patients and caregivers notice changes in a wound's condition between scheduled healthcare visits?
- Could a dressing provide a visible indication of changes in the wound-fluid environment without requiring electronics?
- What materials and safety checks would be needed to explore this idea responsibly?

These questions define the direction of the project; they should not be read as proof that current dressings are ineffective or that Dermasense can detect an infection.

## 3. Proposed Solution

Dermasense is proposed as a layered hydrogel dressing with a pH-responsive colour-indicator region. When wound fluid reaches the sensing region, the indicator may change colour depending on the local pH. The colour response could then be compared with a reference guide developed and tested for the final material.

The proposed concept has four main parts:

| Proposed part | Intended role | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| Transparent protective top film | Protect the dressing and allow the sensing colour to be viewed | Material and performance not selected or tested |
| pH-indicator layer | Provide a visible response to pH changes | Candidate indicators identified; calibration and safety not validated |
| Hydrogel layer | Interact with wound fluid and help maintain a moist dressing environment, depending on its formulation | Material blend and fluid-handling properties not finalised |
| Adhesive border | Hold the dressing in place around the wound | Adhesive type, skin compatibility, and removal performance not tested |

A separate indicator region is being considered so the dye can be contained within the sensing layer rather than freely mixed into the wound-contact layer. This is a design direction, not a validated construction method.

## 4. How the Concept Is Intended to Work

1. The dressing is placed over the wound according to an appropriate wound-care design.
2. Wound fluid reaches the hydrogel and sensing region.
3. The indicator responds to the local pH and produces a visible colour change within its working range.
4. A user observes the change and follows guidance developed with qualified healthcare professionals.

A colour change would indicate a change in the chemical environment around the sensing material. **It would not, by itself, confirm or rule out an infection.** Wound pH can vary with wound type, healing stage, fluid composition, treatments, and other conditions. Research into smart wound monitoring therefore also considers other parameters, including temperature and oxygen levels [1].

## 5. Research Background and Candidate Materials

### Hydrogel materials

Hydrogels are water-containing polymer networks. Depending on their composition and construction, they can absorb wound fluid, retain moisture, and be formed into sheets or films. Their performance is not the same across all formulations, so absorbency, strength, stability, and wound-contact suitability have to be tested for the material actually selected [2].

The current research shortlist includes:

- **Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA):** A synthetic, hydrophilic polymer that can be formed into hydrogel films and blended with other polymers. A PVA-based system is one possible starting point for a sensing layer.
- **PVA/gelatin:** A possible blend to investigate for moisture handling and mechanical properties. Its suitability depends on formulation and testing.
- **Alginate:** A naturally derived polysaccharide that can form gels, including through calcium-ion cross-linking. It is another candidate for a wound-fluid-contact layer.

These are research candidates, not a final recipe for Dermasense. The selected material would need suitable mechanical properties, controlled swelling, stable performance, and biocompatibility evidence for its intended use.

### pH-indicator candidates

Two indicators have been considered in the project research:

| Candidate | Relevant research information | Main question for Dermasense |
|---|---|---|
| **Bromothymol blue (BTB)** | Its usual colour transition is approximately pH 6.0–7.6, from yellow through green near neutral pH to blue at the higher end [3]. A 2025 study investigated BTB in PVA/gelatin-based nanofibrous hydrogels and reported reduced dye release when a complexing agent was used [4]. | Can the indicator be contained safely and produce a clear, repeatable response in the chosen dressing under realistic test conditions? |
| **Phenol red** | A published study developed a colour-responsive alginate/polyacrylamide hydrogel by chemically modifying phenol red so it could be attached to the polymer network, reducing dye leaching in that specific formulation [5]. | Can a suitable formulation provide a useful signal range while meeting safety and stability requirements? |

BTB is a reasonable candidate to investigate first because its transition range is near neutral pH. Phenol red is a relevant alternative because it has been studied in a pH-responsive hydrogel patch. **Neither indicator has been selected as a final, clinically suitable ingredient for Dermasense.** Results from published formulations cannot automatically be applied to a different design.

The most important material questions are the indicator's working range, colour readability, response time, stability, dye leaching, compatibility with other dressing materials, and safety at the intended site of contact.

## 6. Difference from a Standard Hydrogel Dressing

The proposed addition in Dermasense is the pH-responsive colour-indicator layer. The project is investigating whether a dressing could make a change in wound-fluid pH easier to notice without electronics.

A hydrogel alone should not be assumed to prevent infection or to be antimicrobial. Likewise, a colour change does not prove that an infection is present. The concept's value would depend on reliable sensing, safe construction, correct interpretation, and evidence that the signal is useful for the intended users.

## 7. Intended Use Context

The initial use context being explored is wound monitoring after surgery, particularly when the patient is recovering at home. Hospitals, clinics, caregivers, and home-healthcare settings may be relevant stakeholders for future research.

Chronic wounds and diabetic ulcers are possible future areas to study, but the current concept has not been validated for these wound types. The intended users, instructions, and performance requirements would have to be defined for a specific use case before testing a real product.

## 9. Limitations and Safety Considerations

At the time of writing, Dermasense is a research and design concept. The following have not been established:

- A validated, functional prototype or a calibrated colour scale
- Accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, or response time in wound-like conditions
- Reliable performance in the presence of proteins, salts, blood, topical products, or different wound-fluid volumes
- Safe levels of indicator release, skin irritation, sensitisation, or cytotoxicity
- Sterilisation compatibility, packaging integrity, shelf life, or manufacturing consistency
- Clinical performance, regulatory status, or cost-effectiveness

Before any human use could be considered, the design would need an appropriate risk assessment and qualified testing. Depending on the final materials and intended use, this may include pH calibration, testing in simulated wound fluid, dye-leaching and stability studies, mechanical and adhesive testing, biocompatibility assessment, sterilisation validation, packaging and shelf-life testing, and the relevant regulatory and clinical-evidence process.

Dermasense should therefore be described as a **proposed pH-responsive visual wound-monitoring dressing**, not an infection detector. A user should not rely on a colour response to decide whether a wound is infected or to delay professional medical advice.
