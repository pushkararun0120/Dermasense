# Dermasense – Problem Sizing

## 1. Introduction

Problem sizing evaluates the scale, frequency, financial severity, and macro-level drivers of a specific healthcare challenge to determine the need and potential impact of a proposed solution.

For Dermasense—a non-electronic, hydrogel-based wound patch that uses a pH-responsive colour indicator to signal early changes in the wound environment—the core problem centres on the delayed detection of post-surgical wound infections. Because conventional dressings conceal the wound bed and provide no visual warning of early biochemical shifts, infections are frequently identified only after clinical symptoms worsen.

This document outlines the complete problem-sizing framework for Dermasense in India, examining the affected surgical population, post-operative monitoring frequency, clinical and financial severity, annual problem scale, and the PESTEL trends shaping the future of surgical wound care.

---

## 2. Core Problem Definition

### Late Detection of Post-Surgical Wound Infections

Post-surgical wound infections are often detected late because traditional bandages cannot signal early infection, leading to severe clinical complications and significantly higher treatment costs.

Conventional dressings—such as standard gauze and basic adhesive bandages—serve as passive physical barriers. While they protect the incision site and absorb exudate, they offer no visibility into the underlying physiological state of the wound:

- **Lack of early warning signals:** Traditional bandages cannot detect or communicate early biochemical changes in wound fluid, such as the alkaline pH shift commonly associated with bacterial colonisation and developing infection.
- **Delayed clinical intervention:** Without an early visual indicator, patients and caregivers often remain unaware of a developing infection until overt symptoms—such as severe pain, swelling, purulent discharge, or systemic fever—emerge, or until the bandage is removed during a scheduled clinical follow-up.
- **Preventable escalation:** Late detection transforms manageable early-stage wound complications into severe post-operative infections that require intensive medical intervention and costly hospital care.

## 3. Target User Segment (# Users)

### Scope of the Affected Population

Rather than affecting all general healthcare users uniformly, this problem is concentrated within a clearly defined surgical patient profile in India:

- **1 crore surgery patients annually in India:** Approximately 1 crore (10 million) patients undergo surgical procedures each year in India and are exposed to the risk of post-operative surgical site infections during recovery.
- **High-vulnerability patient subgroups (elderly and diabetic patients):** Within this annual surgical cohort, elderly patients and individuals living with diabetes are the most vulnerable. Age-related declines in tissue repair and diabetes-related microvascular and immune impairments significantly increase both the likelihood of developing a post-surgical wound infection and the speed at which complications progress.

Targeting this 1 crore annual surgical patient base—with particular emphasis on high-risk elderly and diabetic patients—defines the primary user segment for Dermasense.

---

## 4. Problem Frequency (# Times / User)

### Post-Operative Risk Window and Monitoring Burden

The frequency of the problem reflects both the duration of post-surgical vulnerability and the recurring hospital visits required to monitor healing incisions:

- **1 to 2 weeks post-surgery infection risk window:** Following a surgical procedure, incision sites remain actively at risk for bacterial colonisation and wound infection for 1 to 2 weeks post-surgery. Continuous vigilance is required throughout this critical recovery phase.
- **Average of 2 hospital visits per patient for wound checks:** During this 1-to-2-week recovery period, patients visit the hospital an average of 2 times specifically for routine wound inspections and dressing changes.

These recurring hospital visits place a substantial time, travel, and logistical burden on recovering patients, families, and outpatient surgical clinics—especially when routine checks only confirm normal healing, or conversely, when an infection develops between scheduled visits and goes unnoticed at home.

---

## 5. Problem Severity (₹ / Time)

### Financial and Clinical Impact per Incident

When a post-surgical wound infection is detected late, the resulting severity is measured across both direct healthcare expenditure and clinical trauma:

### Financial Severity

- **Average cost of ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 per incident:** Treating a post-operative wound infection in India costs an average of ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 per incident. This expenditure covers extended diagnostics, specialized antimicrobial therapies, repeated debridement or dressing procedures, and additional clinical consultations.

### Clinical Severity

- **Prolonged recovery:** Post-operative infections disrupt normal tissue regeneration, immobilising patients for weeks or months beyond expected recovery timelines.
- **Additional surgeries:** Severe or deep-tissue incision infections frequently require surgical re-intervention, wound debridement, or revision procedures.
- **Extended hospital stays:** Patients experiencing post-surgical infection complications often require hospital readmission or prolonged inpatient monitoring, increasing both household financial strain and hospital bed occupancy.

## 6. Annual Problem Size

Combining the affected user segment, post-operative monitoring frequency, and per-incident financial severity establishes the overarching annual problem size in India:

### Problem Sizing Formula

**Annual Problem Size** = (User Segment: Number of Users) × (Frequency: Number of Visits/User) × (Severity: Cost/Time)

### Core Quantitative Inputs

- **Annual Patient & Monitoring Volume:**
  - 1 crore surgery patients annually × 2 hospital visits per patient = **2 crore post-surgical wound-check visits annually**

- **Per-Incident Financial Burden:**
  - **₹20,000 to ₹30,000** per post-operative infection incident

- **Canvas Baseline Representation:**
  - **User & Visit Scale:** 1 crore surgery patients × 2 hospital visits
  - **Incident Cost Scale:** ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 per incident (with a baseline benchmark of ₹20,000 per incident)

This calculation highlights a massive dual burden across the Indian healthcare system: 2 crore annual outpatient hospital visits dedicated to routine post-surgical wound checks, alongside a severe financial penalty of ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 per infection incident whenever complications are caught too late.

## 7. Market Evolution: Boosters (PESTEL Trends)

Evaluating macro-environmental Political, Economic, Socio-Demographic, Technological, Environmental, and Legal (PESTEL) trends reveals two major socio-demographic and epidemiological boosters that are expanding the size and urgency of this problem over time:

- **Aging population with increased surgeries:** As India's demographic profile shifts toward a larger elderly population, the annual volume of age-related surgical procedures (such as orthopaedic, cardiovascular, and general surgeries) continues to rise. Older patients naturally experience slower wound healing and higher susceptibility to post-operative complications, expanding the core at-risk user base.
- **Rising diabetes and immunocompromised patients:** The rapid growth of diabetes prevalence in India, alongside an increasing number of immunocompromised patients, directly amplifies post-surgical risk. Because high blood glucose and weakened immune responses impair normal wound repair and defence against pathogens, a growing proportion of surgical patients fall into the high-vulnerability segment requiring vigilant wound monitoring.

---

## 8. Market Evolution: Setbacks (PESTEL Trends)

Conversely, technological and clinical PESTEL trends present setbacks that help mitigate baseline surgical infection rates:

- **Advancements in surgical techniques:** The growing adoption of minimally invasive surgeries, laparoscopic and robotic procedures, smaller incision sizes, and improved operating-theatre sterilisation protocols reduces tissue trauma and lowers baseline exposure to intraoperative contamination.
- **Broader adoption of preventive antibiotics:** Standardized clinical protocols incorporating prophylactic (preventive) pre- and post-operative antibiotics help suppress early bacterial proliferation at surgical sites.

While these setbacks help reduce intraoperative infection risks, post-discharge wound vulnerability during the 1-to-2-week home recovery period remains a persistent clinical challenge—particularly given rising antibiotic resistance and the growing population of diabetic and elderly surgical patients.

## 10. Conclusion

The problem-sizing analysis demonstrates that post-surgical wound monitoring represents a large-scale, high-consequence healthcare challenge in India. With 1 crore surgical patients annually facing a critical 1-to-2-week post-operative risk window—requiring an average of 2 hospital visits per patient and carrying a treatment cost of ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 per infection incident—the clinical and economic stakes are substantial.

Furthermore, macro-level boosters such as an aging population and rising rates of diabetes and immunocompromise continue to expand the pool of high-risk surgical patients. By addressing the fundamental limitation of traditional bandages through an accessible, non-electronic, pH-responsive colour-changing hydrogel patch, Dermasense is directly positioned to enable earlier infection detection, reduce unnecessary hospital visits, and prevent costly post-operative complications.
