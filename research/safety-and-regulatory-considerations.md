# Dermasense — Safety and Regulatory Considerations

**Project status:** Academic research and product-design concept.  
**Document purpose:** To identify key safety risks and outline the evidence and regulatory questions that would need to be addressed before Dermasense could be considered for use as a wound dressing.  
**Current evidence status:** No validated Dermasense prototype, completed biocompatibility assessment, clinical evidence, product classification, or regulatory approval has been established.

> **Important:** Dermasense is not a finished medical product. Nothing in this document establishes that the proposed materials, indicator, layer design, or finished dressing is safe for skin or wound contact. Do not test the concept on people or use it on a wound. This document is educational project planning, not regulatory or medical advice.

## 1. Safety Position

Dermasense is being explored as a hydrogel dressing concept with a pH-responsive colour-indicator layer. The proposed signal is a change in local fluid pH. Such a change is not, by itself, proof of infection and cannot identify a pathogen, quantify bacterial load, or replace professional wound assessment.

A wound dressing that contacts damaged skin or wound fluid requires careful assessment of its materials, residues, indicator containment, physical performance, microbial control, and intended duration of use. A material that is described as biocompatible in another application cannot automatically be assumed suitable for this particular formulation or exposure.

For the current project, the accurate description is **a proposed pH-responsive visual wound-monitoring dressing concept**. The team has not established that it detects infection, prevents infection, improves healing, reduces hospital visits, or is clinically effective.

## 2. Main Hazards and Questions to Resolve

The table below is a preliminary hazard-identification aid, not a completed risk assessment. Probability, severity, residual risk, and acceptability have not been quantified.

| Potential Hazard or Failure | Why It Matters | Evidence or Control to Investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Indicator leaching | Indicator released from the layer could expose tissue or skin to a substance not intended to contact it. | Evaluate containment, extractables and leachables, exposure under relevant conditions, and a scientifically justified acceptance limit. |
| Local irritation or sensitization | Polymer components, indicator, adhesives, processing residues, or degradation products may cause adverse skin reactions. | Characterize the final formulation and obtain a qualified biological-evaluation plan appropriate to the intended contact. |
| Cytotoxicity or other biological effects | The complete material system may affect cells or tissues even if individual ingredients have prior uses. | Select appropriate biological tests with qualified specialists; do not infer safety from another published formulation. |
| Inadequate fluid handling | Excessive swelling, poor absorption, drying, or fluid retention may make the dressing unsuitable for its intended conditions. | Define requirements and measure swelling, fluid uptake, physical integrity, and relevant transmission properties. |
| Layer separation or material residue | Delamination, tearing, or residues could compromise use or make removal difficult. | Examine layer integrity, flexibility, removal behaviour, and failures after fluid exposure and ageing. |
| Misleading colour interpretation | A colour change may be mistaken for infection, or the absence of a change may provide false reassurance. | Treat colour as a pH-related signal only; study readability and interference; avoid diagnostic instructions unless supported by evidence. |
| Interference or unstable response | Wound-fluid composition, topical products, lighting, material ageing, or pH changes unrelated to infection may affect the displayed colour. | Test likely interferents and define controlled reading conditions; characterize limitations. |
| Contamination or inadequate microbial control | A wound-contact product may become contaminated during production, storage, or use. | Develop an appropriate manufacturing hygiene, bioburden, packaging, and sterilization strategy for the intended product. |
| Sterilization changes the product | A sterilization process can alter polymer properties, indicator colour, adhesive performance, or packaging. | Assess compatibility and validate any proposed process; do not assume sterilization is suitable without evidence. |
| Packaging or shelf-life failure | Moisture, light, oxygen, or package damage may degrade the hydrogel or indicator before use. | Evaluate packaging integrity, storage conditions, photostability, and stability over the proposed shelf life. |
| Manufacturing variability | Variation in thickness, indicator loading, cross-linking, or curing may change colour response and physical properties. | Define critical process parameters and batch-level quality checks; assess repeatability across independently prepared batches. |

Risk controls should be considered in the design itself wherever possible, then supported by documented manufacturing controls, instructions, testing, and residual-risk review. A list of possible hazards is not a substitute for a formal risk-management process.

## 3. Candidate-Material Considerations

The project research currently treats PVA, PVA/gelatin, and alginate-based hydrogels as candidates, with bromothymol blue (BTB) and phenol red as indicators to compare. None has been selected as a final, verified formulation.

### Bromothymol Blue (BTB)

BTB has a nominal transition interval around pH 6.0–7.6 in standard indicator use, but the response interval and colour appearance may change after incorporation into a polymer matrix. A central issue is whether the indicator remains contained and stable during exposure to the intended fluid environment. Leaching, biological compatibility, photostability, repeatability, and colour readability require testing in the actual proposed formulation.

Results reported for a different BTB-containing research dressing do not validate Dermasense.

### Phenol Red

Phenol red is another candidate, with a nominal transition interval around pH 6.8–8.2 in standard indicator use. Research has described its incorporation into a hydrogel system, but this does not establish its suitability for Dermasense. The research notes also flag phenol red’s reported weak estrogen-mimic activity in certain cell-culture contexts; therefore, the exposure scenario, immobilization strategy, leaching, and biological risk assessment require particular care if it remains under consideration.

### Hydrogel Matrix, Adhesive, and Processing Residues

The complete formulation matters. Polymer purity, cross-linking agents, residual solvents or reagents, adhesives, degradation products, and interactions between layers should be considered. Published use of a polymer in wound-care research does not prove that every formulation or manufacturing process is safe for wound contact.

For the current concept, keeping the indicator layer separate from direct wound contact is a design hypothesis to evaluate, not a demonstrated safety control. The team must still determine whether fluid transport, dye migration, and layer integrity are acceptable under relevant conditions.

## 4. Biological Evaluation and Performance Evidence

The biological evaluation plan should be based on the final device design, the tissues contacted, the nature and duration of contact, the materials and processing residues, and the intended use. Qualified specialists should determine which tests and chemical characterization activities are appropriate. Potential topics include:

- Cytotoxicity.
- Skin irritation and sensitization.
- Chemical characterization and extractables/leachables.
- Exposure assessment for released substances and degradation products.
- Material and adhesive compatibility with the intended contact conditions.
- Bioburden and sterilization validation, if the intended product requires it.
- Packaging integrity and shelf-life evidence.

The list is not a universal test prescription. Not every test is necessarily applicable to every design, and additional work may be needed. Test selection should follow a documented risk-based evaluation and applicable requirements. See [ISO 10993-1:2025](https://www.iso.org/standard/10993-1) for the general framework for biological evaluation of medical devices, and [ISO 14971:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/72704.html) for medical-device risk management. The applicable editions and regulatory recognition should be checked when the project advances.

Performance evidence is also separate from biological safety. A formulation may display a colour change and still fail in readability, repeatability, fluid handling, stability, or correct interpretation. Equally, a pH response does not establish diagnostic sensitivity or specificity for infection.

## 5. Risk Management and Design Controls

A future development effort should document at least the following:

1. **Intended purpose:** Target wound type, intended user, setting, expected duration of contact, how the colour is intended to be interpreted, and whether the user is expected to take any action based on it.
2. **Design definition:** Materials, layer arrangement, indicator containment approach, dimensions, adhesive, manufacturing method, packaging, and whether the product is single-use.
3. **Hazard analysis:** Foreseeable hazards, failure modes, possible harms, severity and probability estimates where supportable, risk controls, and residual-risk decisions.
4. **Traceability:** Links between user needs, design requirements, hazards, tests, results, and any proposed claims.
5. **Change control:** A record of formulation, supplier, process, packaging, or intended-use changes, with a decision on which tests need to be repeated.
6. **Quality management:** Controlled procedures, training, supplier controls, batch records, complaint handling, and corrective actions appropriate to the development stage and applicable requirements.

ISO 13485:2016 is an international standard commonly used for medical-device quality-management systems; its applicability and any regulatory obligations should be evaluated for the actual development and manufacturing context. See the [official ISO overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/59752.html).

## 6. Regulatory Considerations for India

The regulatory pathway cannot be determined from the project name or from the fact that it is a hydrogel dressing alone. It depends on the final composition, intended purpose, claims, design, risk, and how the product is intended to be supplied and used.

For an India-focused project, the [Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO)](https://cdsco.gov.in/) and the official [Medical Devices Rules, 2017](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2022/m_device/Medical%20Devices%20Rules%2C%202017.pdf?v=20260514095313) are starting points for regulatory research. The Rules include categories such as surgical dressings within their scope, but this statement does **not** classify Dermasense or establish which permissions apply to this proposed indicator-containing product. The current rules, amendments, applicable schedules, device classification, licensing route, and evidence expectations must be checked against the actual intended use with qualified regulatory advice and, where appropriate, the relevant authority.

Before any future human use or commercialization is considered, seek guidance on:

- Intended-use wording and permissible claims.
- Applicable device classification and licensing or registration pathway.
- Required risk-management documentation and biological evaluation.
- Performance and usability evidence.
- Manufacturing controls and quality-system requirements.
- Sterilization method and validation, where applicable.
- Labelling, instructions for use, storage, shelf life, and warnings.
- Clinical or other additional evidence requirements, if applicable.
- Post-market monitoring and complaint-handling obligations, where applicable.

Other markets may have different requirements. Do not assume that meeting an ISO standard on its own gives regulatory authorization to market a product.

## 7. Claims to Avoid at the Current Stage

Until appropriate evidence supports them, the GitHub repository, presentation, diagrams, and any future website should avoid stating or implying that Dermasense:

- Detects or diagnoses wound infection.
- Prevents infection or sepsis.
- Guarantees early detection or faster healing.
- Reduces hospital visits or treatment costs by a measured amount.
- Is safe, sterile, clinically effective, or approved.
- Provides validated real-time monitoring.

Safer wording for the current stage is:

> **Dermasense is an academic concept for a hydrogel wound dressing with a proposed pH-responsive colour-indicator layer. The concept is intended to explore whether changes in local fluid pH can be displayed visually. Its performance, biological safety, clinical meaning, and regulatory pathway have not been validated.**

## 8. Recommended Next Actions

1. Review the intended purpose and product concept with a wound-care or biomaterials specialist before fixing design requirements.
2. Maintain an initial hazard list and update it as the design changes.

5. Do not conduct human, patient, or wound-contact trials as part of informal student testing. Obtain the relevant expert, ethics, and regulatory review before any study involving people or clinical materials.
