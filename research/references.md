# Dermasense – References

## 1. Introduction

This document contains the main scientific papers, research reviews, official databases, and other sources used to support the Dermasense project.

The references cover hydrogel-based wound dressings, wound-fluid pH, pH-responsive colour indicators, smart wound-monitoring systems, wound-care needs in India, market research, material safety, and regulatory considerations.

The references support the background research and proposed design of Dermasense. Findings from published studies relate to the specific materials and test conditions investigated by their authors and should not be treated as proof that the Dermasense formulation has the same performance.

## 5. pH Indicator Chemistry and Material Considerations

### 5.1 Bromothymol Blue

**Database:** PubChem, National Center for Biotechnology Information.

**Compound record:** Bromothymol Blue, PubChem Compound ID (CID) 6450.

**Link:** https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/6450

**Alternative compound link:** https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/bromothymol_blue

**Relevance to Dermasense:** Provides chemical identity and general information about bromothymol blue as an acid-base indicator. Its commonly reported transition range is approximately pH 6.0–7.6.

### 5.2 Phenol Red

**Database:** PubChem, National Center for Biotechnology Information.

**Compound record:** Phenol Red, PubChem Compound ID (CID) 4766.

**Link:** https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/4766

**Alternative compound link:** https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/phenol-red

**Relevance to Dermasense:** Provides chemical identity and general information about phenol red. Its commonly reported colour transition occurs across approximately pH 6.8–8.2.

The transition ranges listed above are reference values for the indicators themselves. Their actual response, visible colour, and stability must be determined in the final hydrogel formulation and under the intended test conditions.

### 5.3 Phenol Red in Tissue Culture Media Is a Weak Estrogen: Implications Concerning the Study of Estrogen-Responsive Cells in Culture

## 9. Reference Use and Research Integrity

The sources in this document serve different purposes. Peer-reviewed research articles provide evidence about particular materials, methods, and experimental outcomes. Official databases provide chemical or health-related reference information, while government resources provide regulatory information. Commercial market reports provide estimates based on their stated market definitions and methodologies.

When using these sources in the Dermasense documentation:

- Attribute scientific findings to the original authors.
- Use population-specific figures only within the limits of the studies that reported them.
- Do not treat the wider wound-care market as the addressable market for Dermasense.
- Do not assume that results from another hydrogel formulation apply to the proposed Dermasense design.
- Do not describe a pH-responsive colour change as a confirmed diagnosis of infection.
- Add any new source to this list when it is used to support a new technical, scientific, market, or regulatory claim.

The references provide a basis for further research and design decisions. The performance, safety, and suitability of Dermasense must be established through evidence specific to the proposed product.
