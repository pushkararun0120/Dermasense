# Dermasense — Project Overview

## 1. Introduction

Dermasense is an academic product-design project focused on a proposed hydrogel wound dressing with a pH-responsive colour indicator. The idea is to make changes in the wound environment more visible through a simple colour response, without requiring batteries, electronic sensors, or a mobile application.

The project explores how hydrogel materials and pH-sensitive indicators could be combined in a wound dressing intended to support visual wound monitoring, particularly during recovery at home.

Dermasense is currently a research and design concept. The product's performance, safety, and ability to provide clinically useful information have not yet been validated.

## 2. Problem Statement

After surgery, patients may need to monitor their wounds while recovering at home. Changes in a wound's condition may not always be easy for patients or caregivers to assess, and some situations require professional examination.

Follow-up appointments can also involve travel, time, and additional expenses, particularly for people who live far from healthcare facilities.

Dermasense explores whether a conventional wound dressing could provide an additional visual indicator of changes in wound-fluid pH. The goal is to investigate a simple monitoring aid that could complement, rather than replace, professional wound assessment.

The scale of this problem and the potential benefits of the proposed solution require further investigation using reliable clinical and healthcare data.

## 3. Proposed Solution

The proposed Dermasense dressing combines a hydrogel layer with a pH-responsive indicator layer.

The concept follows four main steps:

1. **Fluid interaction:** Wound fluid reaches the dressing's sensing region.
2. **pH response:** The indicator responds to the local pH by changing colour.
3. **Visual observation:** The colour can be compared with a reference scale developed and validated for the final material.
4. **Appropriate follow-up:** A concerning result could prompt the user to contact a healthcare professional.

This process describes the intended design, not a currently validated product workflow.

A change in pH does not confirm that a wound is infected. Similarly, the absence of a colour change cannot establish that a wound is healthy. The concept would require further research before its colour response could be interpreted for clinical use.

## 4. Proposed Product Design

The initial design explores four functional components.

| Component | Intended purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Transparent protective film | Protect the dressing and allow the indicator colour to be observed. |
| pH-indicator layer | Produce a visible response to changes in pH. |
| Hydrogel layer | Interact with wound fluid and help maintain a moist wound environment, depending on the final formulation. |
| Adhesive border | Help secure the dressing around the wound while avoiding unnecessary adhesive contact with the wound bed. |

The final arrangement will depend on the selected materials and experimental results. In particular, the indicator should be contained or immobilized appropriately, and its release into wound fluid must be assessed.

The early design also considers different patch shapes and sizes for different wound locations. These are preliminary design options rather than finalized product specifications.

## 5. Materials Under Consideration

The project has identified several materials for further study.

### 5.1 Hydrogel Materials

**Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA):** A synthetic, hydrophilic polymer that can form hydrogel films. It is a candidate for exploring the dressing's structure and mechanical properties.

**Alginate:** A naturally derived polysaccharide that can form hydrogels under suitable conditions. It is being considered for its potential role in fluid absorption and the wound-contact layer.

**Gelatin:** A protein-derived material that may be combined with PVA to adjust the properties of a hydrogel.

These materials have different strengths and limitations. No final formulation has been selected for Dermasense, and their suitability will depend on performance, safety, stability, and manufacturing requirements.

### 5.2 pH Indicators

Two candidate indicators have been identified:

- **Bromothymol blue (BTB):** An indicator with a typical transition range of approximately pH 6.0–7.6.
- **Phenol red:** An indicator with a typical transition range of approximately pH 6.8–8.2.

These ranges are general chemical properties and do not establish suitable clinical thresholds for wound monitoring. The indicators must be compared under relevant experimental conditions, including simulated wound-fluid exposure, colour visibility, stability, and leaching.

Bromothymol blue is the initial candidate for further investigation, but it has not been selected as a finalized or validated component.

## 6. Intended Users and Potential Applications

The initial concept focuses on exploring wound monitoring during recovery at home.

Potential user groups and settings include:

- Patients recovering from surgery.
- Caregivers helping patients observe their dressings.
- Hospitals and surgical centres evaluating new wound-care approaches.
- Home-healthcare providers.

Other possible future research areas include diabetic ulcers and chronic wounds. These applications are outside the scope of any established capability at the current stage and would require separate evaluation.

Dermasense is not intended to encourage users to diagnose infections or change their treatment based solely on a dressing's colour.

## 7. Main Design Objectives

The project aims to investigate the following:

- Develop a concept for a hydrogel dressing with a visible pH-responsive indicator.
- Explore whether the indicator can be incorporated without unacceptable leaching.
- Study suitable materials for fluid interaction, flexibility, and colour visibility.
- Investigate a simple, electronics-free method of presenting the indicator response.
- Identify the safety, usability, manufacturing, and regulatory requirements for further development.

These objectives will guide material selection and prototype planning. They are not claims that the proposed product has already achieved these outcomes.

## 8. Limitations and Safety Considerations

Several important questions remain unresolved.

**pH interpretation:** Wound-fluid pH can vary for reasons other than infection. It cannot independently establish whether an infection is present.

**Material compatibility:** Each layer must be evaluated for its intended use and type of contact with skin or wound tissue.

**Indicator leaching:** The possibility of the dye entering wound fluid must be investigated. Free dye should not be assumed safe for direct wound contact.

**Colour accuracy:** Lighting conditions, fluid composition, material thickness, indicator concentration, and colour perception may affect interpretation.

**Performance:** Response time, repeatability, stability, absorbency, mechanical properties, and performance after sterilization remain to be tested.

**Clinical and regulatory requirements:** The final product's intended use and claims would determine the appropriate safety assessment and regulatory pathway.

No claim is currently made that Dermasense prevents infection, diagnoses wound complications, improves healing, reduces hospital visits, or is safe for clinical use.

## 9. Development Plan

The proposed development process is divided into four stages.

### Stage 1: Research and Material Selection

Review research on wound-fluid pH, hydrogel wound dressings, and colourimetric indicators. Compare candidate materials and identify the properties required for the intended application.

### Stage 2: Initial Laboratory Prototype

Explore suitable hydrogel formulations and indicator-immobilization methods. Prepare test samples for controlled pH-response experiments.

### Stage 3: Performance and Safety Evaluation

Investigate colour response, repeatability, fluid absorption, stability, and indicator leaching. Identify the required biocompatibility and other safety assessments with appropriate technical guidance.

### Stage 4: Further Validation

Use the laboratory findings to determine whether the concept warrants further development. Any testing involving people or clinical use would require appropriate professional oversight, ethics review where applicable, and compliance with relevant requirements.

These stages are proposed future work; they should not be interpreted as completed development activities.

## 10. Project Status

**Current stage:** Academic research, documentation, and product-concept development.

The project documentation describes the intended product design, candidate materials, possible sensing mechanism, technical limitations, and proposed testing pathway.

A validated working prototype, measured performance results, clinical evidence, and regulatory approval have not been established in the project material available at this stage.

The next priority is to organize the supporting scientific references and develop a testable plan for comparing hydrogel formulations and pH indicators.

## 11. Conclusion

Dermasense explores the possibility of combining a hydrogel wound dressing with a pH-responsive colour indicator to make changes in wound-fluid pH visible without electronics.

The concept offers a direction for investigating simple visual wound monitoring, but significant material, safety, performance, and clinical questions remain.

Further development will depend on reliable research, controlled laboratory testing, and evidence that supports the intended use. Until that evidence is available, Dermasense should be treated as an academic product concept rather than a clinically validated wound-monitoring device.
